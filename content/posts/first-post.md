Where do I find myself at this moment?

In Hong Kong, an amazing city which holds a lot of interest for me! More precisely, in my one-room apartment in a neighborhood called Wan Chai on the Hong Kong Island.

I started working as a postdoctoral researcher in the Department of Mathematics at the University of Hong Kong almost a year ago, in October 2025. At the end of November, I got the exciting news that I was awarded a grant from the Czech Science Foundation. At first, everything seemed rosy: the project is of a type called “Postdoc Individual Fellowship — Outgoing” and funds two years spent at a university abroad and one year back in the Czech Republic.

Then the problems started to crop up. For one thing, I needed to notify the Immigration Department in Hong Kong about my upcoming change of employment and apply for a new visa. To leave enough time for the visa situation to be resolved, I requested that the start of the project be moved to the latest possible date, October 1, 2026.

On the Czech side, the project application was submitted by the Faculty of Information Technology of the Czech Technical University. People from the faculty administration started to get involved and brought up a host of other issues about taxes, salary, health insurance etc. I quickly found myself in a kafkaesque quadrangle between CTU, HKU, the grant agency, and ImmD. Around the beginning of September, I wasn't sure whether the project would launch at all.

Nevertheless, all the major issues got resolved. I obtained a new employment visa permitting me to stay in Hong Kong for the time being. My status at HKU changed from a “Postdoctoral Fellow” to “Visiting Research Associate — unpaid”. And now it even looks like I might receive some salary from my new employer, the Faculty of Information Technology of CTU. In fact, I believe I might be the first person ever to work in Hong Kong supported by the Czech Science Foundation! A more detailed description of the trials and tribulations that I went through will hopefully make it on this blog later on in the form of practical tips for other postdocs who got this particular type of grant. Now it's time to let some of the stress melt away and turn my mind to brighter things.

Here is a list of some of the things I'd like to do in the near future:
- Make a hard push to produce the first mathematical results in the new project
- Facilitate the signing of a cooperation agreement between CTU and HKU
- Help turn HKU Number Theory Days from a small event for a few invited speakers to a larger conference with contributed talks in 2027.
- Write more posts for this blog!

Regarding the last point: For a long time, I thought that there were plenty of people ranting about something on the internet, and adding one more voice to the mix was absolutely pointless. But I'm writing this at a moment when it seems that publishing mathematical articles, which was pretty hard to begin with, could become a lot harder. The nature of the entire mathematical profession might change, with aspects like expository work, communication, and outreach gaining prominence. So when to start a mathematical blog, if not right now? Soon it might be too late.

I want my ideas to reach other people more easily — that's one half of it. But I knew from the beginning that I wanted my blog to have a comment section, so that readers like you could easily reach out to me. The comments are open, get involved!
